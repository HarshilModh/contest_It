## One-line pitch

Contest It turns a New York City summons into real, data-backed odds of
dismissal—and a factual defense draft—using the city’s own hearing outcomes.

## Problem

New Yorkers often pay a summons because contesting it feels like a blind
gamble. The rules are difficult to navigate, and a generic chatbot cannot tell
someone how comparable charges actually ended. Meanwhile, NYC publishes the
outcome of OATH hearings, but those records are too large and technical for
most people to use at the moment they need them.

## What we built

Contest It accepts a photo of a summons, a spoken description, or a manually
entered violation code. Gemini extracts the charge details from the image or
transcript. The app then aggregates historical cases for that charge from NYC
Open Data, calculates the dismissal rate and penalty statistics, and uses
Gemini to explain the result in plain English and draft a short, factual
statement for the hearing. The interface always keeps manual entry available
and clearly states that the result is a statistical summary, not legal advice.

## Data and technology

- **Runtime AI:** Google Gemini for structured summons extraction and the
  plain-English analysis / defense draft.
- **Public data:** NYC Open Data, **OATH Hearings Division Case Status**,
  Socrata dataset `jz4z-kudi`.
- **Statistics:** Computed by our application from OATH records; they are not
  generated by the language model.
- **App:** Next.js, TypeScript, React, and Tailwind CSS; deployed on Vercel.
- **Voice:** Chrome Web Speech API as a thin transcript input, with manual text
  entry always available as the fallback.

## What we would build next

We would add charge-code normalization across agency formatting variants,
filters for decision year and borough, cited links to relevant hearing rules,
Spanish and additional language support, and a privacy-preserving path for
users to save evidence and hearing deadlines. We would also validate the
statistical methodology with legal-aid partners before presenting it beyond a
hackathon prototype.

## Short form description

Contest It tells New Yorkers their actual statistical odds of beating a city
summons. Gemini reads a ticket photo or spoken description, while our app
queries and aggregates real NYC OATH hearing outcomes for the extracted charge
code. It returns a plain-English verdict, sourced dismissal and penalty
statistics, and a factual defense draft. A precomputed cache protects the demo
from unreliable Wi-Fi, and the interface always labels the result as data—not
legal advice.
